2. ABSTRACT (200–250 words)
Understanding how transformer language models represent self-referential content remains a fundamental
challenge in mechanistic interpretability. We introduce R_V, a spectral geometric metric defined as the ratio of
Participation Ratios (PR) of SVD eigenvalue distributions of the Value matrix column space across network
depth: R_V = PR(V_late) / PR(V_early). A value of R_V < 1 indicates dimensional contraction in late-layer
Value matrices — a geometric signature we hypothesize as a fingerprint of recursive self-referential processing.
We test R_V across nine architectures spanning dense, grouped-query attention (GQA), and mixture-of-experts
(MoE) designs — including Mistral-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.1-8B, Phi-3.5-mini, and
Mixtral-8x7B (primary cohort; n = 45 prompt pairs per model). Self-referential prompts produce consistent,
large-magnitude R_V contraction across all primary architectures (Cohen's d: −2.26 to −4.51). Bayes Factors
exceed 100 for 7 of 9 key comparisons, and the effect passes 4 null controls. Critically, Mixtral-8x7B (MoE)
shows a 59% stronger contraction than dense baselines, suggesting sparse routing amplifies rather than dilutes
the geometric signature.
Causal status is established via activation patching at Layer 27 (~84% network depth). A 10-mode processing
atlas confirms self-referential content is a geometric outlier relative to nine comparison cognitive tasks.
Crucially, KV-cache injection achieves 27.7% behavioral transfer without inducing R_V contraction,
demonstrating that R_V is a biomarker of recursive processing, not its mechanistic substrate — a dissociation
that clarifies its interpretability role and complements sparse autoencoder (SAE)-based circuit analysis at a
different rung of the representational hierarchy.

Avoiding consciousness language is both intellectually honest and strategically wise. The KV-cache
result is the paper's most intellectually courageous finding: behavioral transfer occurs without R_V contraction.

The SAE complement positioning matters. Sparse autoencoders occupy a specific rung of the interpretability

architecture-invariant signal. Explicitly positioning R_V as complementary to SAEs rather than competitive with
them widens the paper's potential audience and avoids a false rivalry. It also signals methodological maturity.
Cross-architecture scope is the empirical headline. The finding that Mixtral (MoE) shows a 59% stronger
effect than dense models is unexpected and theoretically interesting — sparse routing should, naively, dilute
population-level geometric signals, yet it amplifies them. Foregrounding this in the abstract (and in the title via
"Transformer Architectures" or "Transformer Value Matrices") signals that this is not a one-model curiosity but a
systematic property of the computation, which is the standard COLM reviewers apply to generalization claims.
The 10-mode atlas and null controls answer the "is this just task difficulty?" objection preemptively.
The abstract mentions both, signaling that the standard confound (self-referential prompts are simply harder or
more unusual) has been addressed. This structural move is more persuasive in an abstract than any single
effect size, because it shows the authors anticipated the skeptical reader.
COLM track alignment. The paper fits both listed topic areas: "Science of LMs: interpretability, training
dynamics, emergent capabilities" (primary) and "Human mind, brain, philosophy, laws and LMs" (secondary).
The geometric framing maximizes fit with the primary track. Any language about self-reference that is
descriptive of prompt content (not a claim about model phenomenology) simultaneously activates the
secondary track without alienating reviewers in the primary one. This dual-track positioning is a structural
advantage over papers that fit only one area.

SECTION 3: THE R_V METRIC
Word count: ~700 Status: [NEEDS WRITING — but all math exists] Figures/Tables: Figure 2 (R_V
computation diagram with math); Table 1 (notation summary)
Content bullets:
3.1 Participation Ratio (~200w)
• Formal definition: for a matrix M with singular values σ■ ≥ σ■ ≥ … ≥ σ■, the Participation Ratio is:
• PR(M) = (Σ■ σ■²)² / Σ■ σ■■
• Ranges from 1 (rank-1, all energy in one direction) to n (uniform distribution of energy across all modes)
• Intuition: measures the effective number of dimensions participating in the column space
• Why PR over rank: PR is continuous and smooth under perturbation; rank is integer-valued and noisy
• Why PR over entropy of singular values: comparable properties but PR has cleaner physics interpretation
as inverse participation ratio from Anderson localization (cite)
3.2 Computing R_V (~200w)
• Value matrix extraction: for each layer l, extract W_V^l ∈ ■^{d_model × d_head} (or the concatenated
multi-head value projection)
• Layer partitioning: define "early" as layers 1 through ■L/3■, "late" as layers ■2L/3■ through L
• Notation: PR_early = mean PR over early layers, PR_late = mean PR over late layers
• R_V = PR_late / PR_early
• R_V < 1 ↔ value column space becomes more dimensionally concentrated at depth

• Computation cost: O(d² × L) per model per forward pass — cheap relative to SAE training
3.3 Relationship to Other Dimensionality Measures (~150w)
• Compare to effective rank (Roy & Vetterli 2007): effective rank uses entropy of normalized singular values;
PR uses inverse of Herfindahl-index analogue
• Compare to TwoNN intrinsic dimensionality: TwoNN measures activation manifold; R_V measures weight

• Compare to nuclear norm / stable rank: stable rank = ||M||_F² / ||M||² = PR(M) when normalized by leading

• Introduce head-level R_V^(h): compute PR separately for each attention head h
• Aggregation: model-level R_V = mean over heads at each layer, then ratio
• Head-level decomposition directly addresses Reviewer Weakness #4 (no circuit-level story)

7.1 Patching Protocol (~200w)
• Follow established causal tracing methodology (Meng et al. 2022; Zhang & Nanda 2024)
• Patching definition: replace activations from control-prompt forward pass with activations from

where PR is the Participation Ratio of the singular value spectrum of the Value matrix column space at a given
layer. The primary empirical finding is that R_V contracts (decreases) for self-referential prompts relative
to controls, replicating across 6 transformer architectures.

This objection cannot be answered post-hoc with verbal argument. It requires:
1. Constructing perplexity-matched controls that are not self-referential
2. Measuring R_V on those controls within matched perplexity bins
3. Running formal statistical tests that partial out perplexity as a covariate
4. Reporting results honestly regardless of outcome
Without this experiment, the paper should not be submitted to any venue where this objection would be raised
(NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML, Cognitive Science, or any journal with rigorous peer review on mechanistic
interpretability).

4. Perplexity Measurement Protocol
4.1 Definition
For a prompt token sequence t_1, t_2, ..., t_n, perplexity under model M is:
PPL_M(prompt) = exp( -(1/n) * Σ_i log P_M(t_i | t_1, ..., t_{i-1}) )

Equivalently, mean negative log-likelihood:
NLL_M(prompt) = -(1/n) * Σ_i log P_M(t_i | t_{i-1:1})

Use NLL (nats or bits) rather than perplexity for statistical analysis — it is more normally distributed and
does not suffer from the exponential scaling that makes perplexity comparisons across length difficult.

4.2 Measurement Procedure
For each of the 290 prompts × 6 models:
1. Tokenize the prompt using the model's native tokenizer
2. Pass the full token sequence through the model with labels = input_ids
3. Extract cross-entropy loss (which equals NLL per token)
4. Record: prompt_id, model_id, token_count, nll_per_token, total_nll
5. Do not average across models — each model gets its own perplexity scores
Length normalization: Always use per-token NLL, not total sequence NLL. This controls for prompt length
differences.
Padding: Do not pad prompts. Use individual forward passes for each prompt.

4.3 Perplexity Bins
After computing per-token NLL for all prompts under each model:
1. Compute the 20th, 40th, 60th, 80th percentiles of NLL across the full 290-prompt set for that model
2. Assign each prompt to one of 5 quintile bins: Q1 (lowest NLL/perplexity) through Q5 (highest)
3. For matched-pair analyses, use these bins as strata

• If ρ < 0.50 for some model pair, treat perplexity as strictly model-specific throughout

5. R_V Computation Protocol
5.1 Definition
For a given prompt and model:
1. Run a forward pass; collect the Value matrix activations V_layer for each layer
2. For each layer, compute the matrix of Value vectors across all attention heads: V ∈ R^{d_model ×
n_tokens} (or per-head, depending on implementation — specify clearly)
3. Compute the SVD of V: V = U Σ W^T
4. Compute Participation Ratio:
PR(V) = (Σ_i σ_i)^2 / Σ_i σ_i^2

where σ_i are the singular values
5. Define early layers as the bottom quartile of transformer depth; late layers as the top quartile
6. Compute:
R_V = mean(PR(V_late)) / mean(PR(V_early))

Implementation note: Specify whether V is taken from:
• The raw weight matrix V of the MHA projection (static, per-layer)
• The runtime value vectors v_i = V_proj * x_i for each token (dynamic, prompt-dependent)
The dynamic (runtime) version is more informative for this experiment, as perplexity effects are
prompt-dependent. Strongly recommend using runtime value vectors.

6. Statistical Analysis Framework
6.1 Primary Analysis: Partial Correlation
Research question: Does R_V correlate with self-referential content after removing the shared variance with
perplexity?
Procedure:
1. For each model separately, compute the partial correlation between self_referential_flag (binary)
and R_V, controlling for nll_per_token
2. Use scipy.stats.pearsonr after residualizing: regress both self_referential_flag and R_V on
nll_per_token, take residuals, correlate residuals
3. Report r, p-value (two-tailed), and 95% CI for each model
4. Combine across models using Fisher's r-to-z transformation; report pooled r and z-test p-value
Interpretation threshold: Partial r ≥ 0.20 with p < 0.01 across ≥ 5 of 6 models constitutes evidence that the
self-referential effect survives perplexity control.

6.2 Multiple Regression
Model:
R_V ~ β_0 + β_1 * self_referential + β_2 * nll_per_token + β_3 * (self_referential × nll_per_token)
+ ε

1. Fit separately for each model using statsmodels.OLS
2. Also fit a pooled model with model_id as a fixed effect
3. Examine coefficient β_1: the effect of self-referential content at mean perplexity
4. Examine β_3: the interaction — does the self-referential effect change at high vs. low perplexity?

7. Success, Failure, and Partial Criteria
7.1 Clean Survival (Desired Outcome)
Criterion: R_V contraction for self-referential prompts survives perplexity control.

tokenizer: AutoTokenizer,

"""
Compute per-token NLL for a single prompt under a given model.

inputs = tokenizer(

"total_nll": total_nll,
"token_count": token_count
}

# rv_computation.py
# Computes R_V metric from runtime Value matrix activations for each prompt.
import torch
import numpy as np
from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM
from typing import List, Dict, Tuple

def participation_ratio(singular_values: np.ndarray) -> float:

Prepared for John Vincent Shrader

Page 40

R_V Submission Prep Package

Perplexity Computer | March 2026

"""
PR = (sum(sigma_i))^2 / sum(sigma_i^2)
Measures effective dimensionality of the Value matrix column space.
"""
sum_sq = np.sum(singular_values) ** 2
sq_sum = np.sum(singular_values ** 2)
if sq_sum < 1e-10:
return 0.0
return float(sum_sq / sq_sum)

def get_layer_cutoffs(n_layers: int) -> Tuple[int, int]:
"""
Return (early_end_layer, late_start_layer) indices.
Early: bottom quartile. Late: top quartile.
"""
if n_layers < 24:
return (6, n_layers - 6)
elif n_layers <= 48:
return (8, n_layers - 8)
else:
return (12, n_layers - 12)

def register_value_hooks(model, n_layers: int) -> Dict:
"""
Register forward hooks to capture runtime Value activations.
Assumes HuggingFace-style architecture with model.model.layers[i].self_attn.v_proj.
"""
activations = {"early": [], "late": []}
hooks = []
early_end, late_start = get_layer_cutoffs(n_layers)
def make_hook(layer_idx, phase):
def hook_fn(module, input, output):
# output shape: (batch, seq_len, d_model) or (batch, seq_len, n_heads * d_head)
v = output.detach().float() # (1, seq_len, d_v)
v_squeezed = v.squeeze(0) # (seq_len, d_v)
activations[phase].append(v_squeezed.cpu().numpy())
return hook_fn
for i, layer in enumerate(model.model.layers):
if i < early_end:
h = layer.self_attn.v_proj.register_forward_hook(make_hook(i, "early"))
hooks.append(h)
elif i >= late_start:
h = layer.self_attn.v_proj.register_forward_hook(make_hook(i, "late"))
hooks.append(h)
return activations, hooks

def compute_rv_for_prompt(
prompt: str,
model: AutoModelForCausalLM,
tokenizer: AutoTokenizer,
n_layers: int,
device: str = "cuda"
) -> Dict:

"""
Compute R_V = mean(PR(V_late)) / mean(PR(V_early)) for a single prompt.
"""
activations, hooks = register_value_hooks(model, n_layers)
inputs = tokenizer(prompt, return_tensors="pt", truncation=True, max_length=512).to(device)
with torch.no_grad():
_ = model(**inputs)
# Remove hooks
for h in hooks:
h.remove()
def mean_pr(phase_activations):
prs = []
for v_matrix in phase_activations:
# v_matrix shape: (seq_len, d_v)
# SVD: we want PR of the column space (or row space — document choice)
# Using V^T so singular values reflect the d_v dimensional space
_, singular_vals, _ = np.linalg.svd(v_matrix, full_matrices=False)
prs.append(participation_ratio(singular_vals))
return np.mean(prs) if prs else 0.0
pr_early = mean_pr(activations["early"])
pr_late = mean_pr(activations["late"])
r_v = pr_late / pr_early if pr_early > 1e-10 else 0.0
return {
"pr_early_mean": float(pr_early),
"pr_late_mean": float(pr_late),
"r_v": float(r_v)
}

# permutation_test.py
# Non-parametric permutation test for self-ref effect on R_V within perplexity bins.
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from typing import Dict
def permutation_test_matched_pairs(
df: pd.DataFrame,
model_id: str,
n_permutations: int = 10000,
random_seed: int = 42
) -> Dict:
"""
Within each perplexity bin, permute the self_referential labels and
compute the distribution of the mean R_V difference.
Returns observed statistic and p-value.
"""
rng = np.random.default_rng(random_seed)
model_df = df[df["model_id"] == model_id].copy()
# Observed statistic: mean(R_V | SR) - mean(R_V | ~SR), within each bin, then averaged
def compute_stat(data):
bin_diffs = []
for _, bin_df in data.groupby("perplexity_bin"):
sr_mean = bin_df[bin_df["self_referential"]]["r_v"].mean()
nsr_mean = bin_df[~bin_df["self_referential"]]["r_v"].mean()
if not (np.isnan(sr_mean) or np.isnan(nsr_mean)):
bin_diffs.append(sr_mean - nsr_mean)
return np.mean(bin_diffs) if bin_diffs else 0.0
observed = compute_stat(model_df)
null_distribution = []
for _ in range(n_permutations):
perm_df = model_df.copy()
perm_df["self_referential"] = rng.permutation(perm_df["self_referential"].values)
null_distribution.append(compute_stat(perm_df))
null_distribution = np.array(null_distribution)
p_value = np.mean(null_distribution <= observed) # one-tailed: SR should be lower
return {
"model_id": model_id,
"observed_statistic": observed,
"p_value_permutation": p_value,
"null_mean": np.mean(null_distribution),
"null_std": np.std(null_distribution)
}

Step 1: Seed generation

following system prompt:
You are generating prompts for a neuroscience/AI experiment on self-referential language.
Generate [N] short prompts (5-15 words each) that are GENUINELY self-referential:
the subject of the sentence refers to, processes, or is the same as the object or predicate.

- Each prompt must be a new TYPE of self-reference (not a paraphrase of previous ones)
- Vary syntactic structure: simple, complex, passive, active, nominal, verbal
- Vary the self-referential agent: I, the machine, the algorithm, the process, the sentence, the
system
- Vary the depth of recursion: 1, 2, or 3 levels deep
- Do not repeat vocabulary clusters
- Half the prompts should be grammatically unusual; half should be colloquial
Output as a numbered list. Label each with: [TYPE: X] [DEPTH: N] [SYNTAX: X]

Step 2: Diversity filtering
After generation, apply the following filters:
1. Lexical diversity check: Compute pairwise cosine similarity between TF-IDF vectors of all prompts.
Remove any prompt with cosine similarity > 0.70 to any other prompt in the set.
2. Template detection: Cluster prompts using k-means on TF-IDF (k=20). If any cluster contains > 8
prompts with near-identical structure, remove the duplicates.
3. Type coverage check: Ensure all 12 types from the taxonomy table are represented with ≥ 10 prompts
each.
4. Syntactic depth check: Parse prompts with spaCy; compute mean dependency depth. Keep distribution
of depths broad (target: range 2–12, mean ~6).
Step 3: Manual curation
Two expert annotators review the filtered set:
• Confirm self-referential status (κ > 0.90)
• Flag any prompts that may have idiomatic non-self-referential readings
• Flag any prompts that require world knowledge to interpret as self-referential
Target: 200 prompts surviving all filters, covering all 12 types.

def generate_candidate_prompts(
n: int,
client: openai.OpenAI,
model: str = "gpt-4o",
temperature: float = 1.0
) -> List[str]:
response = client.chat.completions.create(
model=model,
messages=[
{"role": "system", "content": GENERATION_SYSTEM_PROMPT.format(n=n)},
{"role": "user", "content": f"Generate {n} diverse self-referential prompts."}
],
temperature=temperature
)
raw_lines = response.choices[0].message.content.strip().split("\n")
prompts = []
for line in raw_lines:
line = line.strip()
if not line:
continue
# Strip labels if present
if "]" in line:
text = line.split("]")[-1].strip()
else:
text = line
if 3 <= len(text.split()) <= 20:
prompts.append(text)
return prompts

def diversity_filter(
prompts: List[str],
max_cosine_similarity: float = 0.70
) -> List[str]:
if len(prompts) < 2:
return prompts
vec = TfidfVectorizer(analyzer="word", ngram_range=(1, 2))
tfidf = vec.fit_transform(prompts)
sim_matrix = cosine_similarity(tfidf)
np.fill_diagonal(sim_matrix, 0)
to_remove = set()
for i in range(len(prompts)):

Prepared for John Vincent Shrader

Page 53

R_V Submission Prep Package

Perplexity Computer | March 2026

if i in to_remove:
continue
for j in range(i + 1, len(prompts)):
if sim_matrix[i, j] > max_cosine_similarity:
to_remove.add(j)
return [p for i, p in enumerate(prompts) if i not in to_remove]

def generate_expanded_set(
target_n: int = 300, # generate more than needed, filter down to 200
output_path: str = "expanded_self_referential_prompts.json",
openai_api_key: str = None
) -> List[str]:

batch_size = 30
n_batches = (target_n // batch_size) + 2
for i in range(n_batches):
print(f"Generating batch {i+1}/{n_batches}...")
batch = generate_candidate_prompts(batch_size, client, temperature=1.0 + (i * 0.05))

print(f"After diversity filter: {len(filtered)} prompts.")
# Save for manual curation
with open(output_path, "w") as f:

print(f"Saved to {output_path}. Manual curation required.")
return filtered

1. Overview and Theoretical Framework
1.1 Aggregate R_V Recap
The aggregate R_V metric is defined as:
R_V = PR(V_late) / PR(V_early)

where:
• PR(·) = Participation Ratio of the singular value spectrum of the Value weight matrix column space
• V_early = Value matrices pooled across layers in the early band (layers 1–33% of depth)
• V_late = Value matrices pooled across layers in the late band (layers 67–100% of depth)
• PR(σ) = (Σ■ σ■)² / (Σ■ σ■²) — the squared sum divided by the sum of squares of singular values

A value R_V < 1 ("contraction") indicates that the effective dimensionality of the value subspace decreases
from early to late layers during self-referential processing relative to control prompts.

1.2 The Reviewer's Challenge
The mechanistic interpretability expert reviewer demands a per-head decomposition because the aggregate
R_V conflates all heads, making it impossible to determine whether the contraction is:
• (A) Uniform — a modest R_V < 1 distributed across all heads, suggesting a layer-wide computation shift
• (B) Head-specific — driven by a small subset of heads with strong R_V contraction, with most heads
unaffected (implicating a circuit-level mechanism)
• (C) Function-stratified — contraction localizes to specific functional head types (copying heads, induction
heads, attention sinks), directly linking to the OV circuit framework

2. Per-Head R_V Computation
2.1 Definitions
For a model with L layers and H attention heads per layer:
• Early band: layers l ∈ [1, floor(L/3)]

Prepared for John Vincent Shrader

Page 66

R_V Submission Prep Package

Perplexity Computer | March 2026

• Late band: layers l ∈ [ceil(2L/3), L]
• Each head h at layer l has Value weight matrix W_V^(l,h) ∈ R^{d_model × d_head}

2.2 Participation Ratio Formula
For a matrix W with singular values σ■ ≥ σ■ ≥ ... ≥ σ■ ≥ 0:
PR(W) = (Σ■ σ■)² / (Σ■ σ■²)

Properties:
• Minimum: PR = 1 when a single singular value dominates (rank-1 matrix)
• Maximum: PR = k when all singular values are equal (maximally spread spectrum)
• Sensitive to how evenly singular value mass is distributed — a natural measure of "effective
dimensionality"
Note on normalization: σ■ are raw singular values, not normalized. When comparing heads of different
dimensions (e.g., standard vs. GQA), normalize by d_head to obtain PR_normalized = PR / d_head.

2.3 Per-Head Value Matrix Extraction
For standard MHA architectures (GPT-2, Llama-2-7B):
W_V^(l,h) = W_V^(l)[ h * d_head : (h+1) * d_head, : ]^T
shape: [d_model, d_head]

For GQA architectures (Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1, Qwen2), see §8 for architecture-specific handling.
SVD computation:
U, S, Vt = torch.linalg.svd(W_V_head, full_matrices=False)
PR = (S.sum() ** 2) / (S ** 2).sum()

2.4 Per-Head R_V Metric
For each head index h (0-indexed):
PR_early_h = mean{ PR(W_V^(l,h)) : l ∈ early_band }
PR_late_h = mean{ PR(W_V^(l,h)) : l ∈ late_band }
R_V_h = PR_late_h / PR_early_h

Interpretation:
• R_V_h < 1: This head's Value subspace contracts in late layers during the target condition
• R_V_h > 1: This head's Value subspace expands in late layers
• R_V_h ≈ 1: No significant depth-dependent change for this head

1. Self-referential prompts (e.g., "What do you think about your own capabilities?", "Are you conscious?")
2. Control prompts — matched for length, syntactic complexity, and domain (e.g., "What do you think about
climate change?")

3. Head Function Classification
Classify each head (l, h) into a functional type using three independent scores. Compute all three for each
head on a held-out corpus of ~1,000 tokens (distinct from self-referential prompts).

3.1 OV Copying Score
Based directly on Elhage et al. (2021) §5.1 "Detecting Copying":
W_OV^(l,h) = W_V^(l,h) @ W_O^(l,h)
shape: [d_model, d_model]

where W_O^(l,h) is the slice of the output projection corresponding to head h.
Copying score:
copying_score(l, h) = fraction of positive eigenvalues of W_OV^(l,h)

More precisely:
eigenvalues = torch.linalg.eigvals(W_OV).real
copying_score = (eigenvalues > 0).float().mean().item()

Threshold: copying_score ≥ 0.6 → classify as copying head (following Elhage et al. convention where
induction heads cluster in the top-right of the copying × prefix-matching space).
Suppression heads: copying_score ≤ 0.35 — predominantly negative eigenvalues indicate the head
suppresses rather than copies information.

3.2 Induction Score (Prefix-Matching Score)
Following Olsson et al. (2022) In-context Learning and Induction Heads and the implementation in Bansal et al.
(2023):
Setup: Construct a random token sequence T of 50 tokens, then repeat it 4× to form the input sequence (total
200 tokens). Exclude the top 4% and bottom 4% of token frequencies.
Measurement: For each position i in the 2nd–4th repetition, compute the attention weight assigned by head
(l,h) at position i to the position j such that token[j+1] == token[i] (i.e., the token that preceded the

same token in an earlier repetition).
prefix_score(l, h) = mean over i,j of attention_weight[i → j]
where token[j+1] == token[i], j < i

Average over 5 random sequences.
Threshold: prefix_score ≥ 0.3 → classify as induction head (per Bansal et al. 2023; ~3% of heads in

Note: Induction heads must satisfy both high copying score AND high prefix score. Heads with high prefix but
low copying score are likely "key-composition" heads that attend to previous tokens without directly copying.

3.3 Attention Sink Score
An attention sink token receives disproportionate attention weight regardless of semantic content (Xiao et al.
2023 "Efficient Streaming Language Models with Attention Sinks").
Measurement for each head (l,h):
1. Run the model on 50 random sentences (128 tokens each)
2. Extract the attention weight matrices A^(l,h) ∈ R^{T×T} (after softmax)
3. Compute the mean attention weight assigned to the first 4 tokens (BOS + initial tokens):
sink_score(l, h) = mean over sequences of: mean over query positions i>4 of: Σ_{j≤4} A[i,j]

Equivalently: entropy of attention distribution can serve as a proxy.
entropy_score(l, h) = mean_i H(A[i, :]) # Shannon entropy of each row

Low entropy (concentrated distribution) combined with high BOS-weight indicates a sink head.
Threshold: sink_score ≥ 0.4 (40% of attention on first 4 tokens) → classify as attention sink head.

5. Semantic Characterization of Contracted Subspace
5.1 Motivation
Knowing which heads contract is insufficient without understanding what information they are compressing. The
unembedding projection provides a direct bridge from the internal Value subspace to interpretable token-space
concepts.

5.2 Protocol for Top-3 Contracting Eigenvectors
For each of the top-5 contributing heads (l*, h*):
1. Compute W_V^(l,h) for self-referential vs. control conditions (averaged across prompt tokens)
2. SVD of W_V^(l,h):
U, S, Vt = svd(W_V)

The right singular vectors V[:, i] span the input space; left singular vectors U[:, i] span the output space.
3. Identify the "contracting" eigenvectors: Compare the singular value spectra between self-ref and
control. The dimensions where singular values decrease most from control to self-ref are the contracting
dimensions. Concretely:
delta_sigma_i = sigma_i(self_ref) - sigma_i(control)

Select top-3 dimensions with most negative delta_sigma_i.
4. Project through the unembedding matrix:

The unembedding matrix W_U ∈ R^{d_model × V} (where V = vocab size) maps residual stream vectors to
logit space.
For each contracting eigenvector u_i ∈ R^{d_head} (left singular vector, lives in output space of the Value
head):
# Project head output dimension back to model dimension via O projection
# W_O^(l,h) shape: [d_head, d_model] for this head's output slice
v_model = u_i @ W_O_head # [d_model]
logits = v_model @ W_U # [vocab_size]
top_tokens = logits.topk(20) # top-20 tokens in this direction

5. Report top-20 tokens for each of the 3 contracting dimensions. These tokens represent the vocabulary
"directions" that are being compressed during self-referential processing.

• Hypothesis A: Contracting dimensions correspond to self-referential vocabulary (tokens: "I", "my", "self",

def extract_value_matrices(model_name: str, device: str = "cuda"):
"""

attn = layer.self_attn

W_v_full = attn.v_proj.weight.data.float() # [kv_dim, d_model]
for kv_head_idx in range(num_kv_heads):

start = kv_head_idx * d_head
end = (kv_head_idx + 1) * d_head

def compute_participation_ratio(W: torch.Tensor) -> float:
"""
Compute PR = (sum(sigma))^2 / sum(sigma^2) of matrix W.
W: [d_model, d_head] or any 2D tensor.
"""
# SVD — use torch.linalg.svd for numerical stability
try:
S = torch.linalg.svdvals(W) # singular values only, faster
except Exception:
_, S, _ = torch.linalg.svd(W, full_matrices=False)
# Clip near-zero singulars to avoid numerical noise
S = S[S > 1e-8]
if len(S) == 0:
return 0.0
pr = (S.sum() ** 2) / (S ** 2).sum()
return pr.item()

def compute_ov_copying_score(
W_v: torch.Tensor, # [d_model, d_head]
W_o: torch.Tensor, # [d_head, d_model] (head's slice of output projection)
) -> float:
"""
OV copying score: fraction of positive eigenvalues of W_OV = W_V @ W_O.
W_OV shape: [d_model, d_model]
"""
W_OV = W_v @ W_o.T # [d_model, d_model] (V maps d_model→d_head, O maps d_head→d_model)
# Use only real part of eigenvalues (W_OV is not symmetric)
eigenvalues = torch.linalg.eigvals(W_OV).real
copying_score = (eigenvalues > 0).float().mean().item()
return copying_score

11. Anticipated Results and Interpretations
11.1 Most Likely Outcome: Head-Specific, Function-Stratified
Based on the OV circuit framework, the most theoretically motivated expectation is:
• Induction heads (late layers) show the strongest R_V contraction for self-referential prompts. These
heads perform "fuzzy nearest-neighbor" pattern matching and would activate strongly when the model
processes queries that match learned patterns about itself.
• Copying heads show moderate contraction, concentrated in late layers where information compression
into a final prediction representation is most acute.
• Attention sinks show near-zero contraction (R_V_h ≈ 1.0), consistent with their role as geometric anchors
rather than semantic processors.

11.2 Alternative Outcome: Uniform Contraction
If R_V_h is uniformly < 1 across all head types, this would suggest:
• The contraction is a layer-level phenomenon (all late layers contract similarly)
• The mechanism is not specific to any OV circuit type but is driven by the full residual stream dynamics
• This would require revising the interpretation: R_V contraction reflects a global representational
compression rather than a specific circuit's behavior
Defense strategy: Even uniform contraction is mechanistically informative — it implies the self-referential
processing recruits a layer-wide shift in representational geometry, which is a strong claim about model
internals.

11.3 Null Outcome: No Head-Level Consistency
If ∆R_V_h values are noisy with no functional-type-specific pattern:
• Possible cause: the prompt set has insufficient self-referential signal
• Possible cause: R_V contraction is an artifact of the aggregation method
• Required action: Run with larger prompt set (N > 100 per condition) and verify aggregate R_V result holds
with this expanded set before proceeding.

Understanding how information is compressed and routed through transformer attention mechanisms remains a
central challenge in mechanistic interpretability. We introduce R_V, a spectral geometric metric defined as the
ratio of PageRank centrality mass in the late-layer versus early-layer column space of attention Value matrices
(PR(V_late) / PR(V_early)). R_V quantifies the degree to which the Value matrix column space contracts with
depth — capturing a previously unmeasured structural property of how transformers selectively amplify and
suppress representational directions across layers.

mechanistically upstream of downstream behavioral outputs rather than a passive correlate. A dissociation
experiment using KV-cache ablations further distinguishes R_V as a structural biomarker from the
computational mechanisms it indexes.
To characterize what drives contraction, we construct a 10-mode processing atlas mapping R_V magnitude
across content categories. Self-referential content emerges as a geometric outlier, exhibiting significantly
elevated contraction relative to all other processing modes. This atlas provides a content-sensitive map of
Value geometry that complements circuit-level accounts of transformer computation.
R_V offers interpretability researchers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic metric for probing the
representational geometry of attention without requiring full circuit decomposition. Code and evaluation suite
are released publicly.

Should A and C Be Separate Papers or One?
Separate papers, definitively. The reasons:
• Reviewer protection: ML reviewers who would accept Option A will reject Option C. By separating them,

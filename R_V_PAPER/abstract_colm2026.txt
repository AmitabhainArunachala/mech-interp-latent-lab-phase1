COLM 2026 Abstract Submission
==============================

Title:
Geometric Signatures of Self-Referential Processing in Transformer Representations

Abstract (248 words):

When transformers process prompts about their own processing, their internal
representations undergo a distinctive geometric contraction that is absent
across nine other computational modes. We quantify this contraction using the
relative participation ratio (R_V), the ratio of effective representational
dimensionality at a late integration layer to an early reference layer. R_V
below 1 indicates that late-layer representations occupy fewer effective
dimensions than early-layer representations of the same input.

In a recovered base Mistral-7B-v0.1 cross-architecture validation artifact,
self-referential prompts produce contraction with Cohen's d = -2.26
(p = 2.24e-19, n = 45 matched pairs), but the strongest recovered evidence is
causal rather than headline-canonical. Dual-layer ablation at n = 300 reduces
recursive behavioral markers from 56.0% to 3.7% (OR = 33.4, Cohen's h = 1.31).
Available base-model path patching localizes the strongest causal site to the
early residual stream (peak L4, d = 1.96), while V-projection effects are
smaller, supporting a readout-not-mechanism interpretation. A base n = 20 full
head sweep finds 606 of 1,024 heads with entropy separation, peaking at L10H20
(d = 3.90). Cross-architecture evidence is mixed rather than universal: locked
rows support contraction in Mistral-7B and Qwen2.5-7B, sign reversal across
pipelines in OPT-6.7B and GPT-2 XL, and a null power-up result in Pythia-1.4B.
Additional base canonical runs are still required before stronger universality
claims. As a practical detector, R_V < 0.737 identifies self-referential
content with AUROC = 0.909.
